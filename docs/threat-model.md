# yacana threat model

What an attacker can and cannot do against the mechanism as built, with the figures measured in this
repository. Attack costs below are *measured upper bounds on security*: they say how expensive the best
attack we implemented or timed is, not that no cheaper one exists. Mainnet operations (fee path, launch,
hardening pass) are a later plan; there is no external audit (owner's decision).

## Actors

Rational miners with modified clients; other miners (griefing, withholding); the sequencer (delay,
censor, order — cannot forge); web users (phishing, XSS, malicious RPC); the supply chain and toolchain.

## The guarantee

**One fresh accepting UltraHonk transcript of the work circuit `W` per ticket, each proof point in its one
reduced encoding.** Tickets from one witness still differ in its unconstrained cells (below). The ticket is
`Poseidon2(DOM_TICKET ∥ proof[0..410])` over the *entire* proof, and the claim verifies that exact proof
in-circuit against the pinned `W_VK` with deployment-bound public inputs (`domain`, epoch seed, epoch,
`Poseidon2(DOM_SECRET, secret, recipient)`, nonce, `out`). Nothing about a ticket can be learned before the last
transcript element exists.

| threat | defence | measured |
|---|---|---|
| Ticket grinding without proving | digest over the whole proof; exact-VK recursion; deployment-bound inputs; determinism and mutation tests; proof-layout manifest | early abort saves only serialisation: `construct_proof` is 94.9–95.2 % of `bb prove` wall clock; tampering any of the 410 fields fails to prove (410/410 native, 8/8 phases in-browser) |
| Re-encoded proof points | the in-circuit verifier's Goblin decoder also accepts a base-field coordinate `c < 2^254 − q` spelled `c + q` (barretenberg#1607), and each spelling hashes to its own ticket; `claim` refuses a proof unless every coordinate of its 36 points is reduced: limbs of 136 and 118 bits, compared limb-wise below q, at offsets generated from the layout manifest (`yacana_miner/src/proof_points.nr`). Any prover must emit reduced coordinates, as bb's serialiser does, or its claims fail at simulation, before any fee | `test/encoding.nr` (the fixture and q − 1 pass; the last point's x or y written as q, a high limb above q, a carried low limb and a wrapped high limb are refused); TXE: a winning claim with a point written as q is refused; `proof-layout.test.ts` (the starts pinned three ways and against the fixture's real points); `bb-verify.test.ts` (native bb refuses the alias at decode) |
| Same-witness re-proof through unconstrained cells | none at the app layer (a bb property): cells no relation constrains, the gate-less rows (≈ 110k in W's 2^18) and, since W has no lookups, its lookup read counts and tags; changing one changes a commitment made before β/γ, so z_perm, sumcheck and the PCS are redone | **not executed** (needs a patched prover); estimated from phase timers at ≈ 54.5 % of an honest prove, an estimated yield of ≈ 1.83 tickets per prove-equivalent for that strategy — an estimate, not a bound; the owner deferred the executed measurement (roadmap) |
| Amortisation (setup, fixed-base tables, witness MSMs) | accepted: bounded *transcript* cost per ticket, not equal hardware cost | — |
| Cross-deployment replay | `domain = Poseidon2(DOM_DEPLOY, chain_id, rollup_version, miner, VERSION)` in the inner public inputs: a fork or upgrade that keeps chain id, address and state cannot reuse work or re-spend tickets | rejected by the verifier in the live suite |
| Double claim / replay | full-width nullifier `Poseidon2(DOM_NULL, digest)` | duplicate nullifier rejected in the live suite |
| Bearer secret | the commitment is `Poseidon2(DOM_SECRET, secret, recipient)`, so a leaked (proof, secret) can only pay the recipient chosen when mining; the secret still dies with its epoch | rejected by the verifier when the recipient differs (live suite) |
| Issuance / load shocks | at most `N` accepted claims and `N × REWARD` per epoch by construction; retarget clamped to [¼, 4] | 10× / 100× / 1000× shocks converge in 2 / 4 / 5 epochs (simulator); 8 concurrent winners against `N = 4`: 4 accepted, 4 reverted in public paying gas (live) |
| Hashrate collapse | anyone may `roll()` after `T_MAX` (×4) | from 2^118, a ÷100 collapse recovers in 5 epochs; from 2^122 the target saturates at 2^128 − 1 and the cadence floors at `N / H` — liveness is kept, cadence is not |
| Withholding / time-warp | clamp + count-capping: stretching an epoch lowers the withholder's YACA/hour; stalling to `T_MAX` needs > 75 % of hashrate | simulated with a strategic miner |
| Roll-seed and closing-digest pre-mining | **no full fix exists; accepted with a bound.** The claim that closes an epoch decides the next seed (`H(seed_e, e+1, closing_digest, now)`). A miner at `N − 1` holding several winning tickets can compute the seed each would produce, pre-mine against each candidate, and submit the one whose continuation gave it the most wins; it pays for the discarded candidates and risks someone else closing first while it waits. Expected extra claims per epoch `≤ min(N, s/(1−s) · p_{e+1}/p_e)` (`s` its hashrate share, `p` the win probability per proof; ≈ 0.25 at a 20 % share and flat difficulty); a censoring sequencer removes the bound. `now` (the slot timestamp) forces a non-sequencer miner to hedge across inclusion slots, which multiplies its cost, while a sequencer-miner needs no guess: kept on purpose, codex would drop it. The escape-hatch seed is public from `opened_at + T_MAX`. Bitcoin has the same structure (selfish mining). Fine at `N = 24`; at `N = 4` one pre-mined claim is a quarter of an epoch, testnet economics only | mean-field simulator over up to 10 slots (`retarget.test.ts`), not an adversarial search |
| Stale claim reverted in public | expected race (§2.4): fee lost, ticket unspent. A claim also caps its own life at `CLAIM_TTL_SECONDS` after its anchor block (`set_expiration_timestamp`), so a claim anchored during an epoch that closed long ago expires in the mempool instead of reverting; the PXE frees a dropped tx's pending delivery index on its next sender sync, whereas a *reverted* claim keeps it until the tx is FINALIZED on L1 (tens of minutes; the web miner rebuilds its chain view from the chain and resumes, or pauses until finality if that does not unblock it) | observed live (`lessons/phase-5.md`); expiration asserted in the live suite |
| Closing-claim gas | the Nth claim also retargets and opens the next epoch (≈ 25 % more public gas); a limit estimated from a non-closing state runs out of gas, reverts, pays and leaves the count at `N − 1` | found live; every miner declares the network's per-tx maximum (`claimGasLimits`) |
| Sponsored-fee griefing | any sponsor that pays for arbitrary calls is drainable by public reverts: premature `roll()`, claims sequenced after a close, or stale claims mined against an older, easier epoch. The TTL bounds the last one to epochs open within `CLAIM_TTL_SECONDS`; the rest is fee policy: the mainnet fee path must be self-paid or a sponsor with per-account quotas and an allowlist (`docs/roadmap.md`) | testnet only: the public sponsored FPC |
| Deployer premine / fair launch | a deployment nobody knows about can be mined alone for as long as its deployer likes (a slow premine keeps difficulty flat), and everything the AVM exposes (addresses, slot timestamps) is choosable or predictable long before the launch, so any seed derived from it alone can be pre-mined for the whole notice period by whoever picks it (a plain `launch()` mixing the launcher's address was tried and is *worse* than a public seed: the first launcher's branch invalidates everyone else's stockpile). Measures: (1) epoch 0 does not exist until `launch()`, allowed only after the announced `launch_at` plus the reveal window (mainnet: one day of notice, a 10-minute window) and only with the token bound before `launch_at` (so the deployer cannot stall it); (2) a RANDAO-style lottery: anyone commits `Poseidon2(DOM_LAUNCH, 1, miner, sender, preimage)` before `launch_at` (bound to the deployment and the address, so a seen commitment cannot be cloned into more addresses), reveals inside the window, and `seed_0 = Poseidon2(DOM_SEED, genesis_seed, 0, mix, now)` where the mix is the *sum* of `Poseidon2(DOM_LAUNCH, 2, miner, sender, preimage)` over the reveals: hidden until each preimage is out (the public commitments use tag 1 and say nothing about it), and commutative (order changes nothing: `k` commitments give `2^k` branches, not `k!` orders). Plainly: a participant keeps a one-bit option per commitment (reveal or withhold), and each candidate seed costs its own pre-mining, so **the head start is bounded by the reveal window only if at least one honest reveal lands late and uncensored**; with no honest reveal the seed depends only on values its author knew all along and the head start is the whole notice, and a sequencer can censor reveals and pick the launch slot. This is RANDAO's known limit; no fundless bond can compel a reveal. It still beats a public seed (symmetric pre-mining for the notice) whenever anyone honest takes part, so the launch announcement should ask miners to commit and reveal late. A quiet deployment can still be mined privately, as with any proof-of-work launch: only the announced deployment is legitimate | TXE: claims and rolls before launch refused; launch before the window closes, unbound or twice refused; commit twice / after launch_at refused; reveal outside the window refused. Noir unit tests: phases, order-independent mixing, contribution ≠ commitment, seed changes with every reveal |
| Deployment invariants | `bind_token` refuses the zero address and any token whose `get_minter()` is not this miner; `launch()` refuses an unbound deployment and `roll()` an unlaunched one (otherwise anyone could roll an unbound instance to a saturated target before it can mint); `bound_token()` and `work_vk_hash()` let anyone check the bound token and the pinned VK against the announcement; the initial target stays a constructor argument (tests mine at easy targets) and is part of the announcement | TXE: wrong minter, zero token and unbound roll rejected; Noir unit tests for the notice rule |

| Timestamps | `opened_at` / `now` come from the sequencer within protocol bounds; durations are minutes, second-level skew is noise; `open_epoch` asserted in both the private anchor and public execution | same-block roll + claim refused at simulation (TXE), stale in public (live) |

## Accepted assumptions

- No malleation of an accepting `W` transcript cheaper than the estimated re-proof exists (the external
  audit that would own this question is not planned).
- The BN254 KZG ceremony.
- Hosted dApps see what the tab sees.

## Out of scope here

Mainnet fee path and launch, external-wallet connection, native mining through aztec-accelerator, the
executed re-derivation through unconstrained cells — all on `docs/roadmap.md`.
